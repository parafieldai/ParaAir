1. Who we are and what this policy covers

1.1 Parafield Inc., a Delaware corporation (“Parafield”, “we”, “us”, “our”), makes ParaAir. ParaAir is a macOS app, a command-line tool and a file system extension. It shows cloud storage as a drive in Finder and streams file pieces on demand.

1.2 This policy explains how we handle claims that material stored with, or made available through, ParaAir infringes someone’s copyright. It covers:

(a) ParaAir Cloud, our paid storage subscription, where Parafield stores files for its customers with our storage providers;

(b) any sharing feature of ParaAir Cloud, such as share links, if and when we offer one; and

(c) the ParaAir website at https://paraair.parafield.ai, and any domain that later replaces it.

The Team plan is planned but not for sale yet. Where this policy mentions Team workspaces, those rules apply once Team plans are offered.

1.3 This policy forms part of the ParaAir Terms of Service at /terms (the “Terms”) and the ParaAir Cloud Service Terms at /cloud-terms (the “Cloud Terms”). Section 1.7 of the Terms sets the order of precedence.

1.4 This policy applies during any invite-only beta of ParaAir Cloud, during free trials, and after general launch.

1.5 We respect the rights of copyright owners. We also respect our customers’ privacy. ParaAir Cloud is private storage. We do not monitor or scan customer files for copyright infringement, and US law does not require us to (17 U.S.C. 512(m)). We act on notices we receive under this policy.

1.6 We do not charge for processing notices or counter-notices. We cannot give legal advice to senders or to our users, and we cannot decide who owns a copyright. Disputes about ownership or fair use belong in court.

2. Where Parafield does not host the files

2.1 Own storage mode. ParaAir can connect to a storage bucket that a customer rents from another provider, such as Amazon S3 or Backblaze B2. In that mode, files go straight from the customer’s Mac to the customer’s own account with that provider. Parafield does not store them, host them or have access to them, and cannot remove them. Please send your notice to that storage provider. If you send us a notice about files in own storage mode and we can tell that is the case, we will reply to say we cannot act on it.

2.2 Copies on a user’s own devices. ParaAir keeps a local cache of file pieces on the user’s Mac. Users can also pin files for offline use. When we remove or disable a file under this policy, we act on the copy that Parafield stores. We cannot reach copies on a user’s own computer or copies the user has made elsewhere.

2.3 Open-source code. The ParaAir client source code is published under the Apache License 2.0 at https://github.com/parafieldai/ParaAir. It includes code from JuiceFS, which is also licensed under Apache-2.0, with attribution in the repository. Those licenses govern the code. This policy covers material that users store or share, not the license terms of the code. If you believe the ParaAir source code itself infringes your copyright, send a notice to our agent in Section 3. If the code is hosted on a third-party platform such as GitHub, you can also use that platform’s own process.

4. How to send a copyright notice (United States)

4.1 To be effective under the Digital Millennium Copyright Act (17 U.S.C. 512(c)(3)), your notice must be in writing, be sent to our agent, and include all of the following:

(a) Signature. A physical or electronic signature of the copyright owner or of a person authorized to act for the owner. A typed full name at the end of an email can serve as an electronic signature.

(b) The work. Identification of the copyrighted work you say has been infringed. If one notice covers several works at a single location, you may give a representative list of those works.

(c) The material. Identification of the material you say is infringing, with information reasonably sufficient for us to find it. See 4.2.

(d) Your contact details. Your name, postal address, telephone number and email address.

(e) Good-faith statement. A statement that you have a good-faith belief that the use of the material is not authorized by the copyright owner, its agent or the law.

(f) Accuracy and authority statement. A statement that the information in your notice is accurate and, under penalty of perjury, that you are the copyright owner or are authorized to act on the owner’s behalf.

4.2 Helping us find the material. ParaAir Cloud files are private. We cannot search every customer’s files for a title, and we will not do so. Details that help us find the material include:

  • the full share link URL, if the material was shared through a ParaAir Cloud link;
  • where you found the material and how it pointed to ParaAir Cloud;
  • the account email address or workspace name, if you know it; and
  • file names and folder paths, if you know them.

If we cannot find the material from the information you give us, we will tell you and ask for more. To check a notice, we may open the specific files it identifies, and only to confirm that they match the notice.

4.3 Incomplete notices. If your notice substantially includes items (b), (c) and (d) above but is missing something else, we will promptly try to contact you to complete it (17 U.S.C. 512(c)(3)(B)(ii)). We may not act on a notice that does not substantially comply with Section 4.1.

4.4 Before you send a notice. Please consider whether the use might be fair use or otherwise permitted by law. Courts have held that a copyright owner must consider fair use before sending a takedown notice. If you are not sure whether material infringes your rights, consider talking to a lawyer first. Sending a knowingly false notice can make you liable for damages (see Section 7).

5. What we do after a valid notice

5.1 We act quickly. When we receive a notice that complies with Section 4, we remove or disable access to the identified material expeditiously.

5.2 How we disable access. When we act on a notice, we:

(a) disable any share link or other means by which the identified material is made available to other people; and

(b) block access to the identified file itself, so that no one, including the account holder, can open, stream or download it from ParaAir Cloud.

(c) In serious cases, we may also restrict the account under Section 8.

We generally keep a disabled file in storage rather than delete it, so that we can restore it if a counter-notice succeeds. The account holder may still delete a disabled file.

5.3 We tell the account holder. We take reasonable steps to promptly notify the account holder whose material we removed or disabled (17 U.S.C. 512(g)(2)(A)). For a Team workspace, we notify the member who uploaded or shared the material, where we can tell who that is, and the workspace owner and admins. We normally include a copy of the notice. That copy may include the sender’s name and contact details, because the account holder needs them to respond.

5.4 We keep records. We record each notice, the action we took and any counter-notice. We use these records to apply the repeat-infringer policy in Section 8. We keep them for 3 years after the related account closes.

5.5 Good-faith removal. We remove or disable material in good faith in response to notices. The law protects us from liability to users for doing so, even if the material is later found not to infringe (17 U.S.C. 512(g)(1)), so long as we follow the counter-notice steps in Section 6.

5.6 Transparency. We may send copies of notices to the Lumen database (lumendatabase.org), or publish summary statistics, with personal contact details removed.

6. Counter-notices

6.1 If material you stored or shared was removed or disabled and you believe this happened because of a mistake or misidentification, you can send a counter-notice to our agent in Section 3. To be effective under 17 U.S.C. 512(g)(3), it must include all of the following:

(a) your physical or electronic signature;

(b) identification of the material that was removed or disabled, and where it appeared before (for example the share link, file name or folder path);

(c) a statement, under penalty of perjury, that you have a good-faith belief that the material was removed or disabled as a result of a mistake or misidentification of the material; and

(d) your name, address and telephone number, and a statement that: - you consent to the jurisdiction of the US federal district court for the judicial district where your address is located or, if your address is outside the United States, any judicial district in which Parafield may be found; and - you will accept service of process from the person who sent the original notice, or that person’s agent.

6.2 Before you send a counter-notice. We will send your counter-notice, including your name and contact details, to the person who sent the original notice. They may use that information to sue you. You may want to talk to a lawyer first.

6.3 What we do with a valid counter-notice.

(a) We promptly send a copy of your counter-notice to the person who sent the original notice. We tell them that we will restore the material, or stop disabling access to it, in 10 business days (17 U.S.C. 512(g)(2)(B)).

(b) We restore the material, or stop disabling access to it, no sooner than 10 and no later than 14 business days after we receive your counter-notice (17 U.S.C. 512(g)(2)(C)).

(c) We will not restore the material if, before then, our agent receives notice from the original sender that they have filed a court action seeking an order to restrain you from infringing activity relating to the material on our service.

6.4 “Business day” in this policy means Monday to Friday, excluding US federal public holidays. We count days in US Pacific Time, where our agent is located.

6.5 If restoring is not possible. We hold a disabled file for 90 days after we notify the account holder, or until any counter-notice process under this Section 6 has ended if that is later. We do this even if the file would otherwise be deleted under our normal recovery and deletion windows. After that, the file stays disabled and is treated like any other file in the account. If the account holder deletes the file, or the account has closed for an unrelated reason, we may not be able to restore it.

6.6 Notices under non-US law. The steps in this Section 6 apply to notices made under the US DMCA. For notices made only under other laws, see Section 10.

7. False claims

7.1 Under 17 U.S.C. 512(f), anyone who knowingly and materially misrepresents that material is infringing, or that material was removed or disabled by mistake or misidentification, may be liable for damages. Those damages include costs and attorneys’ fees incurred by the alleged infringer, by the copyright owner or its licensee, or by Parafield.

7.2 Both notices and counter-notices include statements made under penalty of perjury.

7.3 If a sender repeatedly sends notices that turn out to be false, abusive or made in bad faith, we may review that sender’s later notices more closely before acting, and may report the abuse.

8. Repeat-infringer policy

8.1 Our policy. In appropriate circumstances, we suspend or terminate the accounts of users who are repeat infringers (17 U.S.C. 512(i)(1)(A)). We apply this policy to every account, including paying Personal accounts and Team workspaces. We enforce it in practice, not only on paper. We accommodate and do not interfere with standard technical measures used by copyright owners (17 U.S.C. 512(i)(1)(B)).

8.2 Strikes. An account receives one strike each time we remove or disable material in response to a valid notice under this policy. A notice counts as one strike, even if it lists many files. An account does not receive a strike, and any strike already recorded is removed, if:

(a) the sender withdraws the notice;

(b) we restore the material after a valid counter-notice and do not receive notice of a lawsuit in time under Section 6.3(c); or

(c) we find that the notice was mistaken, invalid or targeted the wrong account.

8.3 Termination after repeated strikes. We terminate an account that receives three strikes within any 12-month period. Strikes older than 12 months no longer count toward this limit, but we keep the record.

8.4 Faster action. We may suspend or terminate an account after fewer strikes, or without strikes, where:

(a) the account appears to be used mainly to store and distribute infringing material, for example by posting share links on public indexing or piracy sites;

(b) a court has found that the account holder infringed copyright using our service; or

(c) the account was created to get around an earlier termination.

8.5 Warnings. Each time an account receives a strike, we email the account holder (and, for a Team workspace, its owner and admins). The email says how many strikes the account has and what happens next.

8.6 Team workspaces. Workspace owners and admins control workspace content and are responsible for acting on notices we forward to them. We record a strike against the member account that uploaded or shared the material, where we can tell who that is. Where we cannot tell, we record the strike against the workspace. If members of one workspace together receive five strikes within any 12-month period, we may suspend or terminate the workspace after notice to its owner.

8.7 What termination means.

(a) We close the account and cancel its subscription, so no further charges are made. Apart from the read-only access in (c), access to ParaAir Cloud ends.

(b) Material disabled under this policy is not returned.

(c) For other files in the account, we give the account holder 30 days of read-only access to download them, unless a law or court order prevents it. After that, we delete them from our active systems within 30 days.

(d) We refund the unused part of any prepaid subscription period. The refund goes to the original payment method through Link, Stripe’s reseller that sold the subscription.

(e) A terminated user may not open a new account or use someone else’s account. We may close any account created to get around a termination.

8.8 Disputing a strike. If you think a strike is wrong, reply to the strike email and explain why. A counter-notice under Section 6 is the formal route for removed material. If we find a strike was recorded in error, we remove it.

8.9 Own storage mode. Strikes apply to ParaAir Cloud accounts. They do not apply to files in a customer’s own storage bucket, which Parafield does not host. That storage provider’s own policies apply.

9. Share links

9.1 ParaAir Cloud is designed as private storage, not a file-distribution service. It does not offer public share links today. If we add them, Sections 9.2 and 9.3 apply.

9.2 If we offer share links, we can disable any link in response to a notice. We may limit or turn off sharing for an account that has received strikes.

9.3 Our Acceptable Use Policy at /acceptable-use does not allow posting ParaAir Cloud share links on public indexing, piracy or link-aggregation sites. Doing so may lead to faster action under Section 8.4.

10. Notices from outside the United States

10.1 We accept notices from anywhere. Copyright owners outside the United States can use the process in Section 4. We do not require a US address or a US copyright registration to act on a notice.

10.2 Other laws. For notices made only under the law of another country, we assess the notice under the law that applies and our Terms. We may ask for more information, or for a court order, before acting.

10.3 Language. Please send notices and counter-notices in English.

11. Other kinds of complaints

The copyright agent handles copyright matters only. For other issues:

  • Child sexual abuse material. Do not send it to us, even as evidence. Report it to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children at CyberTipline.org or 1-800-843-5678, and email abuse@parafield.ai with its location only. We report apparent child sexual abuse material to NCMEC as US law requires (18 U.S.C. 2258A).
  • Intimate images shared without consent. Email abuse@parafield.ai with the subject “Intimate image”. We remove reported intimate images shared without consent within 48 hours of a valid report (AUP 8.3 lists what a valid report contains).
  • Trademark, privacy, defamation or other unlawful content. Email abuse@parafield.ai.
  • Law-enforcement requests. See the ParaAir Law Enforcement Guidelines at /law-enforcement.
  • Account and billing questions. Email support@parafield.ai.
  • Anything else. Email contact@parafield.ai.
