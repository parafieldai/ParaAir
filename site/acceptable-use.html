1. About this Policy

1.1 This Acceptable Use Policy (the “Policy”) sets out what you may not do with ParaAir Cloud and the other ParaAir services run by Parafield Inc. (“Parafield”, “we”, “us”, “our”). It also explains how to report abuse, what we do when the rules are broken, and how to appeal our decisions.

1.2 Parafield Inc. is a Delaware corporation. Our postal address is 18333 Bothell Way NE, Apt 330, Bothell, WA 98011, United States. You can reach us at contact@parafield.ai.

1.3 This Policy is part of the ParaAir Terms of Service (the “Terms”, at /terms). If you use ParaAir Cloud, it is also part of the ParaAir Cloud Service Terms (the “Cloud Terms”, at /cloud-terms). Words defined in the Terms or the Cloud Terms have the same meaning here. Section 1.7 of the Terms sets the order of precedence.

1.4 “You” means the person or organization that uses the Services. For a Team workspace, “you” also means each owner, admin, member and viewer in that workspace. Team plans are not available yet. The rules for Team workspaces apply once they are offered.

1.5 “Services” means:

  • (a) ParaAir Cloud, our paid managed storage subscription, including the file index, read gateway, uploads, sharing features (if offered), file versions and deleted-file recovery;
  • (b) ParaAir accounts and sign-in;
  • (c) the ParaAir website, at https://paraair.parafield.ai, and any domain that replaces it;
  • (d) our download and update services; and
  • (e) our support channels.

1.6 “Content” means files, file names, folder names, metadata and anything else you store, upload or share through the Services.

2. Where this Policy applies

2.1 ParaAir Cloud. This Policy applies in full. Parafield stores your Content on infrastructure we operate or contract for, so we must respond to illegal content on our systems.

2.2 ParaAir with your own storage. When you connect ParaAir to storage you hold with another provider, your files go straight from your Mac to that provider. Parafield never receives, stores or hosts them. Your contract with that provider, including its acceptable use policy, governs what you store there. This Policy still applies to your use of Parafield’s own services, such as ParaAir accounts and sign-in, our website, downloads and support. We cannot see, remove or restore files in your own storage. Please send reports about those files to the storage provider.

2.3 Open-source code. The ParaAir app, command-line tool and file system extension are open source under the Apache License 2.0. Nothing in this Policy limits your rights under that license to use, copy, modify or distribute the code. ParaAir includes code from JuiceFS, also licensed under the Apache License 2.0; see the NOTICE file that ships with ParaAir. This Policy governs how you use Parafield’s Services. You may use a modified client with ParaAir Cloud, as Section 7.4 of the Terms allows, but no client, modified or not, may be used to break the rules in Section 4.

2.4 Beta features. This Policy applies in full to any beta or early-access feature of the Services.

3. Prohibited content

You may not use the Services to store, upload, share or link to any of the following.

3.1 Child sexual abuse and exploitation. We have zero tolerance for:

  • (a) child sexual abuse material (“CSAM”), meaning any visual depiction of a minor engaged in sexually explicit conduct;
  • (b) any content that sexualizes minors, including drawn, animated, computer-generated or AI-generated depictions, whether or not it is illegal where you are; and
  • (c) any content or activity that grooms or entices a minor, or that advertises or facilitates child sex trafficking.

Section 7 explains how we report this material.

3.2 Malware and harmful code. No viruses, ransomware, spyware, trojans, worms, botnet components, credential stealers, exploit kits or other code meant to damage systems, disrupt them or gain unauthorized access, when the purpose is to spread it or use it to cause harm. This includes using the Services to host malware payloads, phishing kits or command-and-control files.

Narrow exception: you may keep malware samples for defensive security research, teaching or incident response if they are private, never shared through share links or with the public, stored in a password-protected archive or clearly labeled, and never served or run from the Services. We may still remove samples if one of our infrastructure providers requires it.

3.3 Infringing content. No content that infringes someone else’s copyright, trademark or other intellectual property rights, or that misuses trade secrets. Examples include pirated software, cracked license keys and unauthorized copies of films, music, books or games kept for distribution. This Policy does not stop you from storing copies you are lawfully entitled to keep. Copyright complaints follow the process in our Copyright and DMCA Policy (the “Copyright Policy”, at /copyright), including counter-notices. Section 9.6 explains our repeat-infringer policy.

3.4 Intimate images shared without consent. No sexual or intimate images or videos of a real person shared, or stored for sharing, without that person’s consent. This includes sexual deepfakes of real, identifiable people and any use of intimate images for threats or extortion (“sextortion”). Privately storing intimate images of yourself, or of adults who consented, is not prohibited. We remove reported non-consensual intimate images within 48 hours of a valid report. Section 8 explains how to report them.

3.5 Other illegal content. No content that is illegal to store or share under laws that apply to you or to us. Examples include:

  • (a) content that promotes or supports terrorism or violent extremism;
  • (b) offers to sell illegal drugs, weapons or other illegal goods or services;
  • (c) stolen personal data, stolen payment card or bank data, stolen login credentials, or other data obtained by unauthorized access;
  • (d) forged or counterfeit documents; and
  • (e) technical data or software exported or shared in breach of export control or sanctions laws.

3.6 Harassment, threats and doxxing. No content shared or used to threaten, harass, intimidate, stalk or bully anyone. No publishing of someone’s private information, such as a home address, identity documents or financial details, without their consent in order to harm or expose them. No content that incites violence, and no content that attacks people based on a protected characteristic when it is shared to harass or incite. Most Content in ParaAir Cloud is private, so this rule mainly concerns what you share, file and folder names other people can see, and conduct inside Team workspaces.

3.7 Fraud and deception. No phishing pages, scam material, fake invoices, or content that impersonates a person or organization in order to deceive.

3.8 Regulated data we have not agreed to hold. Unless we have agreed in writing:

  • (a) do not store protected health information as a HIPAA covered entity or business associate (we do not currently sign business associate agreements);
  • (b) do not use ParaAir Cloud as part of a payment card data environment that must meet PCI DSS; and
  • (c) do not store data that requires special government handling controls, such as ITAR technical data or US Controlled Unclassified Information.

4. Prohibited conduct

4.1 Using ParaAir Cloud as public file hosting, a CDN or a link farm. ParaAir Cloud is a drive for you and the people you work with. It is not a public download site or a content delivery network. You may not:

  • (a) serve website assets, app downloads, software updates, game assets, media streams or other files to the public, or embed or hotlink files from the Services in websites, apps or feeds;
  • (b) post share links in public places, such as forums, social media, search-indexed pages or link directories, for mass distribution, or create share links in bulk for that purpose;
  • (c) run a link farm, link directory, “leak” site or file-locker service, or reward anyone for uploading files or for driving downloads;
  • (d) use ParaAir Cloud as the storage back end for a service you offer to other people; or
  • (e) resell, sublicense, rent or white-label access to the Services without our written agreement.

Sharing files with specific people, such as clients, friends, family or your team, is allowed.

4.2 Getting around quotas, transfer allowances and limits. Each plan includes a storage quota, a monthly transfer allowance at full speed (three times the plan’s storage), reduced speed after that (about 100 Mbit/s), and a capped recovery area for deleted and replaced files. We do not charge overage fees. In return, you may not:

  • (a) open several accounts or workspaces to get more storage or full-speed transfer than your plan includes;
  • (b) share one Personal account among several people, or let people who are not members of your Team use a member’s seat, to avoid per-member pricing;
  • (c) tamper with, spoof or bypass metering, authentication, rate limits or the reduced-speed limit, including through a modified client;
  • (d) use the deleted-file recovery area, file versions or any other space outside your quota as extra storage, for example by deleting and restoring files over and over;
  • (e) sign up repeatedly for free trials, promotions or invitations under different identities; or
  • (f) reach the Services through anything other than the ParaAir clients or the interfaces we document in order to avoid limits.

Reaching a limit in normal use is not a violation. When you use up your full-speed transfer allowance, we slow transfers. We do not suspend an account for that alone.

4.3 Security testing without permission. You may not probe, scan or test the vulnerability of the Services, or break or bypass any security or authentication measure, without our permission. You may not access, or try to access, another user’s account, files or data. You may not use the Services to attack anyone else’s systems.

We welcome good-faith security research. You may test against your own account if you follow these rules: do not access, change or delete anyone else’s data; no denial-of-service or load testing; no social engineering or phishing of our staff or users; stop and report as soon as you find an issue; and give us reasonable time to fix it before you disclose it. Send reports to contact@parafield.ai with the subject “Security”. If you follow these rules in good faith, we will not take legal action against you or ask law enforcement to investigate you for that research. This promise covers only Parafield. Our infrastructure providers, such as Cloudflare and Backblaze, have their own rules for their systems. Do not test their systems themselves.

4.4 Scraping and automated access. You may not scrape, crawl or harvest data from our website, sign-in pages or APIs. You may not collect information about other users. You may not create accounts by automated means. These uses are allowed:

  • (a) search engines indexing our public website as our robots.txt file permits; and
  • (b) automating your own account through the ParaAir app, the command-line tool or any interfaces we document, within published rate limits.

4.5 Interfering with the Services. You may not:

  • (a) overload or disrupt the Services, for example with denial-of-service attacks, request floods or deliberately malformed requests;
  • (b) upload files designed to exhaust our resources, such as decompression bombs aimed at our systems;
  • (c) disable, bypass or weaken any security feature; or
  • (d) introduce code into our servers or infrastructure.

Studying, modifying and sharing the open-source ParaAir client is allowed (Section 2.3). This section protects our servers and infrastructure. It does not restrict what you do with the client’s source code.

4.6 Spam. You may not use share invitations, Team invitations or any other messaging feature to send spam or unsolicited bulk messages.

4.7 Account misuse. You may not:

  • (a) give false identity or billing information, or impersonate anyone;
  • (b) sell, transfer or rent your account;
  • (c) open a new account after we have terminated you under this Policy, unless we agree in writing;
  • (d) use the Services if you are under 13, or, if you are under 18, without the agreement of a parent or guardian, as Section 3 of the Terms describes; or
  • (e) use the Services if you are located in, ordinarily resident in, or organized under the laws of a Restricted Location as defined in Section 26.2 of the Terms, or if you are on a US government restricted-party list, or use the Services to evade sanctions.

4.8 Export control. You are responsible for following export control and sanctions laws for the Content you store or share. The ParaAir client uses standard encryption, and its source code is publicly available. Section 26 of the Terms has more on export controls and sanctions.

4.9 Helping others break these rules. You may not help, encourage or allow anyone else to do anything this Policy prohibits.

5. Team workspaces

Team plans are not for sale yet. This section applies to Team workspaces once Team plans are offered.

5.1 Team owners and admins control the content of their workspace. They can add and remove members, change roles and delete content. They are responsible for making sure members follow this Policy.

5.2 Every member, whatever their role, must also follow this Policy personally.

5.3 When we receive a report about content in a Team workspace, we may first ask the owner or admins to resolve it. We will not do this when the report concerns child safety or a threat to someone’s life or safety, when an owner or admin is the subject of the report, or when the law prevents it.

5.4 We act at the narrowest level that solves the problem: a single file or share link first, then a member, and only then the whole workspace. We suspend a whole workspace only when the violation is severe or widespread, or when the owners or admins took part or did not act after we asked them to.

5.5 Disputes between members, such as who owns a file, are for the workspace and its owner to resolve. We follow the instructions of the workspace’s owners and admins about their workspace’s content, except where this Policy or the law requires otherwise.

6. How we learn about and review possible violations

6.1 No scanning of file contents. We do not scan the contents of files stored in ParaAir Cloud, and we do not use hash matching or other automated tools to look for illegal material in them. We do not routinely monitor Content. US law does not require us to scan for child sexual abuse material (18 U.S.C. 2258A(f)). If we decide to start scanning, we will update this Policy before it begins. This is not a promise that we will never look at Content. Section 6.5 explains when we may.

6.2 How we find out. We may learn about a possible violation from:

  • (a) reports from users or the public (Section 8);
  • (b) notices from rights holders, child-safety hotlines, other organizations or authorities;
  • (c) court orders and other legal process;
  • (d) notices from our infrastructure providers; and
  • (e) signals from running the Services, such as unusual traffic volumes, request patterns that suggest hotlinking or public distribution, mass account creation or attacks.

6.3 Automated tools. We use automated tools to limit request rates, detect attacks on the Services and measure transfer against plan allowances. We do not use automated tools to find illegal content in stored files.

6.4 Human review. A person makes or confirms every decision to remove Content or to suspend or terminate an account. The only exception is an automatic action to protect the Services from an attack in progress; a person reviews those actions afterward.

6.5 Access to your files. ParaAir Cloud is not end-to-end encrypted, so we are technically able to read stored files. Only authorized Parafield staff may look at Content, and only when needed to handle a report, comply with the law, protect someone’s safety, protect the Services, or help you when you ask us to.

6.6 Our standard. We act diligently, objectively and in proportion to the problem. We take into account the rights and legitimate interests of everyone involved, including freedom of expression and privacy.

7. Child safety and reporting to NCMEC

7.1 We have zero tolerance for child sexual abuse and exploitation on the Services. We do not scan file contents for this material (Section 6.1). We act on reports and on the other information described in Section 6.2.

7.2 When we obtain actual knowledge of facts or circumstances showing apparent child sexual abuse material, apparent child sex trafficking or apparent enticement of a minor on the Services, we report it to the CyberTipline of the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (“NCMEC”) as soon as reasonably possible, as 18 U.S.C. 2258A requires. A report may include the content and information about the account involved, such as names, email addresses and billing information we hold. NCMEC may share reports with law enforcement in the United States and other countries.

7.3 We remove or disable access to the material and terminate the accounts involved. We usually do not warn the account holder first. We may never notify them if notice could put a child at risk or interfere with an investigation.

7.4 As 18 U.S.C. 2258A(h) requires, as amended by the REPORT Act, we preserve the reported content and related information for one year after we make the report. We store it securely and limit access to the few people who need it (18 U.S.C. 2258B(c)). We keep it longer if law enforcement asks through valid legal process. These preservation duties override the normal recovery and deletion periods in the Cloud Terms.

7.5 If you come across this material: do not download, copy, screenshot, forward or send it to us, even as evidence. Instead:

  • (a) report its location (the share link, or the file path and workspace) to abuse@parafield.ai with the subject “Child safety”;
  • (b) report it directly to NCMEC at CyberTipline.org or 1-800-843-5678;
  • (c) outside the United States, you may also report to a national hotline listed at inhope.org; and
  • (d) if a child is in immediate danger, call your local emergency number first.

  • (a) the share link, or the file name, path and workspace;
  • (b) the account or workspace involved, if you know it;

  • (iii) your contact details; and
  • (iv) a typed signature, such as your full name at the end of the email.

The 48 hours in Section 3.4 start when we receive a valid report.

We handle child safety, threats to life or safety, intimate images, and active malware or phishing first. We then tell you what we decided.

8.5 Your identity. We generally do not tell the reported user who made a report. Exceptions:

  • (a) copyright notices, which we forward to the user under the counter-notice process in 17 U.S.C. 512(g);
  • (b) when the law requires disclosure; and
  • (c) when your identity is strictly necessary to explain our decision.

8.6 Emergencies. If anyone is in immediate danger, contact local emergency services before you contact us.

8.7 Misusing the reporting process. Do not send reports you know are false, or send them to harass someone. A person who knowingly misrepresents that material is infringing can be liable for damages under 17 U.S.C. 512(f). If someone often sends reports that are clearly unfounded, we may warn them and then stop processing their reports for a period.

9. Enforcement

9.1 What we may do. If you break this Policy, we may:

  • (a) warn you;
  • (b) ask you, or your workspace’s owners or admins, to remove Content;
  • (c) remove or disable access to specific Content;
  • (d) disable a share link, or sharing for the account;
  • (e) limit transfers or features;
  • (f) suspend the account or workspace for a period;
  • (g) terminate the account and the Terms;
  • (h) report the matter to NCMEC, law enforcement or other authorities, where the law requires or allows;
  • (i) preserve information, where the law requires or allows; and
  • (j) refuse future service.

9.2 How we decide. We choose a response in proportion to the problem. We consider:

  • (a) how severe the harm is, and what kind;
  • (b) whether the Content or conduct is illegal;
  • (c) whether it looks deliberate or accidental;
  • (d) any history of violations;
  • (e) how many people are affected, including other members of a Team;
  • (f) the risk to the Services and to our infrastructure providers; and
  • (g) whether a less restrictive step would solve the problem.

Most first problems that are not severe lead to a warning or removal of the specific Content.

9.3 When we act right away. We may act immediately, without a warning first, for:

  • (a) child sexual abuse or exploitation;
  • (b) a credible threat of violence;
  • (c) intimate images shared without consent;
  • (d) malware or phishing being actively spread;
  • (e) attacks on the Services or on others;
  • (f) an account that appears compromised;
  • (g) a legal requirement; or
  • (h) a demand from an infrastructure provider that gives us no time to warn you.

9.4 Notice to you. We tell you what we did, why, and which rule applies, by email to your account’s email address. The notice also explains how to appeal (Section 10). Where practical we tell you before we act, and otherwise no later than when the action takes effect. We may delay or withhold notice when:

  • (a) the law prohibits it, for example under a court order or a nondisclosure order under 18 U.S.C. 2705(b);
  • (b) notice could put a child or anyone else at risk, or compromise an investigation;
  • (c) the violation is an attack or spam; or
  • (d) we do not have your contact details.

9.5 Access to your files after enforcement.

  • (a) Content removal. Only the specific Content is affected.
  • (b) Suspension. We usually leave the account read-only so you can download your lawful files. We do not do this when the suspension concerns child safety, active malware or phishing, an attack, or a legal requirement.
  • (c) Termination for a violation. You get 30 days of read-only access to download your lawful files, matching the period after cancellation in the Terms, unless one of the exceptions in (b) applies or the law requires us to keep or delete specific Content. After that, we delete Content as the Cloud Terms describe, except anything the law requires us to preserve.
  • (d) Fees. If we close your account because you materially breached the Terms, the Cloud Terms or this Policy, we refund the unused part of any prepaid period, unless we closed it for fraud, child sexual abuse material or sanctions reasons, or the law requires otherwise.

9.6 Repeat copyright infringers. In appropriate circumstances, we terminate the accounts of users who repeatedly infringe copyright, as 17 U.S.C. 512(i) requires. Section 8 of the Copyright Policy sets out how we count strikes and when we terminate an account.

9.7 Repeated violations of other rules. If an account repeatedly stores or shares Content that is clearly illegal, we will warn it and then suspend it for a reasonable period, or terminate it.

9.8 Compromised accounts. If an account appears to be compromised, we may suspend it, limit it or require you to sign in again to protect you and others. This is not a finding that you broke this Policy.

9.9 Our infrastructure providers. ParaAir Cloud runs on Cloudflare and Backblaze B2. These providers have their own acceptable use policies. They can suspend our whole service because of one customer’s abuse. We may therefore need to act quickly to meet their requirements, and we may share information with them as needed to resolve an abuse report about our account.

9.10 No waiver. If we choose not to act on a violation once, that does not mean we accept it, and it does not limit what we may do later.

10. Appeals

10.1 You can appeal if we removed or restricted your Content, suspended or terminated your account, or declined to act on your report.

10.2 How to appeal. Reply to our notice, or email contact@parafield.ai with the subject “Appeal”, within six months after we tell you of the decision. Include your account email, the decision you are appealing, why you think it is wrong, and anything that supports your view. Appeals are free.

10.3 Who reviews it. A person reviews every appeal. The decision is never made only by automated means. Where we can, someone who was not involved in the original decision does the review.

10.4 Timing and outcome. We aim to decide within 14 days. We tell you the result and the reasons. If we got it wrong, we reverse the decision without undue delay and restore your Content or account where we can. We cannot restore Content that has already been permanently deleted; we will tell you if that is the case.

10.5 Special cases.

  • (a) Copyright. Copyright takedowns are challenged by counter-notice under Section 6 of the Copyright Policy, not through this appeal process.
  • (b) Legal orders. If we acted because a court or authority ordered us to, challenge the order with the body that issued it.
  • (c) Child safety. You may appeal a child-safety termination. An appeal does not withdraw a report already made to NCMEC, and we will not restore Content we reported.

10.6 Other options. If you still disagree after an appeal, you may use the dispute process in the Terms: informal resolution (Section 23), small claims court where your claim qualifies, or individual arbitration (Section 24). Nothing in this Policy limits rights you have under consumer protection law.

11. Law enforcement and government requests

11.1 The Stored Communications Act (18 U.S.C. 2701 and following) protects stored Content. We disclose Content to a government agency only under a search warrant, or in the emergency and NCMEC cases in Section 11.2. We disclose other records only under the legal process the law requires.

11.2 We may disclose information without legal process in an emergency involving danger of death or serious physical injury, when we believe in good faith that the law permits it, and to NCMEC as described in Section 7.

11.3 We preserve information when we receive a valid preservation request. Under 18 U.S.C. 2703(f), that is for 90 days, renewable for another 90 days.

11.4 We tell users about requests for their information unless the law prohibits it, a court order prevents it, or notice would create a risk of harm.

11.5 The ParaAir Law Enforcement Guidelines are at /law-enforcement. Send requests to contact@parafield.ai, as the Guidelines describe.
