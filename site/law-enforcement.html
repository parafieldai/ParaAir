1. About these guidelines

1.1 These guidelines explain how Parafield Inc. handles requests from government agencies, courts and law-enforcement authorities for information about people who use ParaAir and ParaAir Cloud. Section 6 covers requests in private legal cases, such as civil subpoenas.

1.2 Parafield Inc. is a Delaware corporation. Our address is 18333 Bothell Way NE, Apt 330, Bothell, WA 98011, United States.

1.3 These guidelines are written for agencies and others who send us legal process. ParaAir users can read how we handle their information in the ParaAir Privacy Policy at /privacy.

1.4 These guidelines describe our practices. They do not create rights that anyone can enforce against Parafield, and they do not waive any objection we may have to a request.

2. Terms used in these guidelines

  • Parafield, we and us mean Parafield Inc.
  • ParaAir means the ParaAir app for Mac and the ParaAir website.
  • ParaAir Cloud means Parafield’s paid cloud storage service for ParaAir.
  • Own-storage mode means using ParaAir, free of charge, with storage the user gets from another provider.
  • Account means a ParaAir Cloud account. Each account is identified by an email address.
  • Workspace means the storage space in ParaAir Cloud that holds an account’s files. Each workspace has a workspace ID.
  • User means a person who uses ParaAir or ParaAir Cloud.
  • Contents means the files a user stores in ParaAir Cloud, including earlier versions and recently deleted files, together with their file names, folder names and folder paths.
  • Basic subscriber information and other non-content records are described in section 5.
  • Legal process means a subpoena, court order, search warrant or other order issued under the law.

3. What information we have

3.1 ParaAir Cloud. For a ParaAir Cloud account, we may hold:

  • the email address on the account;
  • workspace records, such as the workspace ID, the plan and the storage used;
  • the billing records we hold, such as subscription status and the dates and amounts of charges and refunds;
  • a record of each subscription agreement: the time, the plan, the price, the version of the terms shown and the app version;
  • sign-in session times;
  • monthly transfer totals;
  • file metadata, such as file sizes, created and modified times and version history;
  • file names, folder names and folder paths; and
  • the contents of files stored in ParaAir Cloud.

We do not keep IP addresses.

3.2 Own-storage mode. In own-storage mode, files go directly between the user’s Mac and the storage provider the user chose. They never reach Parafield. We do not hold those files, their names or their folder structure. The app sends us no usage data. Requests for that data should go to the user’s storage provider.

3.3 Payment card data. ParaAir Cloud subscriptions are sold through Link, Stripe’s reseller service, which is the merchant of record. Stripe and Link hold payment card numbers and other payment details. Parafield does not. Requests for payment details should go to Stripe.

3.4 Deleted data. Deleted and replaced files can be recovered for 30 days on the Personal plan. After that, they are deleted. When a plan ends, the workspace becomes read-only for 30 days, and then its files are deleted. We keep no separate backups of file contents. Once data is deleted, we generally cannot recover it. Logs are kept for limited periods. If you need data kept, send a preservation request (section 9) promptly.

3.5 Where data is stored. ParaAir Cloud is offered only to people and businesses in the United States. File contents are stored in the United States.

4. The law that applies

ParaAir Cloud provides computer storage to the public. Under the Stored Communications Act (18 U.S.C. 2701 to 2713), it is a “remote computing service”. The Act generally bars us from disclosing stored contents to anyone, and customer records to a government entity, except as the Act allows.

5. What we require

5.1 Basic subscriber information. This means the email address on the account, the workspace ID, the date the account was created and its length of service, the plan, and sign-in session times. We require a valid subpoena, as 18 U.S.C. 2703(c)(2) allows, or a court order or search warrant.

5.2 Other non-content records. This means records such as charge, refund and dispute history, file sizes and dates, version history, storage and transfer records, and access logs. We require a court order under 18 U.S.C. 2703(d), or a search warrant.

5.3 Contents. We require a search warrant issued by a court with jurisdiction under the Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure or an equivalent state warrant procedure. We require a warrant even where the statute would allow a subpoena or court order. We treat file names, folder names and folder paths as contents.

6. Civil requests

6.1 The Stored Communications Act does not allow us to disclose contents in response to a civil subpoena or other request from a private party. We direct the requester to the account holder, who has access to the files.

6.2 If we receive a valid civil subpoena or court order for other account records, we notify the user before we respond, where the law allows, so that the user can object. We disclose those records only as the law allows.

6.3 Civil process must be served formally (section 18.4).

7. Requests from outside the United States

7.1 Foreign authorities must use a mutual legal assistance treaty, letters rogatory, or a process available under an executive agreement under the CLOUD Act (18 U.S.C. 2523). We review each request under US law and, where relevant, the law of the requesting country.

7.2 In an emergency involving danger of death or serious physical injury, a foreign authority may contact us as described in section 8. We will respond as US law allows.

8. Emergencies

8.1 If we believe in good faith that an emergency involving danger of death or serious physical injury to any person requires disclosure without delay, we may disclose information related to the emergency, as 18 U.S.C. 2702(b)(8) and (c)(4) allow. We disclose only the minimum needed.

8.2 Emergency requests must come from a law-enforcement or other government official. Email contact@parafield.ai with the subject line “Emergency disclosure request”. Send it from an official government email address and include:

  • your name, title and agency, and a phone number where we can reach you;
  • what the emergency is and who is at risk;
  • why the normal legal process would take too long; and
  • the information you need, the account identifier (email address or workspace ID) and the time range.

8.3 We may contact the agency to confirm that a request is genuine. After an emergency disclosure, we may ask the agency to follow up with legal process.

9. Preservation

9.1 When a government entity asks under 18 U.S.C. 2703(f), we preserve the records it specifies for 90 days. If the entity renews the request before the 90 days end, we preserve them for another 90 days. We extend a preservation once.

9.2 A preservation covers records that exist when we receive the request. It does not cover data created later, and it cannot bring back data that has already been deleted (section 3.4).

9.3 Preserving data is not disclosing it. We disclose preserved data only under the legal process in section 5.

9.4 Send preservation requests the same way as other requests (section 18). Identify the account and the records to be preserved.

10. Child safety

10.1 When we obtain actual knowledge of apparent child sexual abuse material, or of facts or circumstances indicating apparent child sex trafficking or enticement of a minor, we report it to the CyberTipline of the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC), as 18 U.S.C. 2258A requires.

10.2 We preserve the reported material for one year after the report, as 18 U.S.C. 2258A(h) requires. We disable access to it and close the accounts involved.

10.3 We do not scan file contents.

10.4 We do not tell the user about a report when the law prohibits it, or when telling them could put a child at risk or harm an investigation.

10.5 To get more information about a report, use the legal process in section 5.

11. Notice to users

11.1 We notify the affected account holder before we disclose their information, so that they can seek legal protection. We do not give notice when:

  • a law or court order prohibits it, such as an order under 18 U.S.C. 2705(b);
  • notice would create a risk of death or serious physical injury, or of harm to a child;
  • the request concerns child sexual exploitation; or
  • it is an emergency under section 8.

11.2 Outside these cases, an agency’s request that we not notify the user is not enough on its own. To delay notice, obtain an order under 18 U.S.C. 2705(b), or tell us which law prohibits notice.

11.3 We may ask an agency to justify a non-disclosure order, and we may challenge orders that have no end date. When a restriction on notice ends, we notify the user.

12. How we review requests

A designated person at Parafield reviews every request, with outside legal advice where needed. We check that each request is genuine and legally valid. We reject requests that are invalid or overbroad, ask for clarification where a request is unclear, and disclose only what the request lawfully requires. We keep a record of each request, our review and our response.

13. Workspaces run by organizations

Plans for organizations (Team workspaces) are planned but not yet available. When they are offered, the organization will control the data in its workspace. For that data, we will ask the requester to seek it from the organization first, where the investigation allows.

14. Costs

We may seek reimbursement of our reasonable costs of responding, where 18 U.S.C. 2706 or other law allows.

15. No backdoors

We do not give any government direct or indirect access to our systems, encryption keys or user data in bulk. We have not built backdoors into ParaAir or ParaAir Cloud, and we will not build a mechanism that gives a government such access.

16. National security requests

We report on national security requests only in the ways US law allows, including the reporting ranges in 50 U.S.C. 1874.

17. Transparency report

We will publish a transparency report once a year, starting with the first calendar year in which we receive a request. To the extent the law allows, it will show how many requests we received by type of legal process, whether they came from US or foreign authorities, how many led to disclosure of any data, and how many users we notified.

18. How to send a request

18.1 Send requests by email to contact@parafield.ai with the subject line “Law enforcement request”. For emergencies, follow section 8.

18.2 You can also send requests by post to Parafield Inc., 18333 Bothell Way NE, Apt 330, Bothell, WA 98011, United States.

18.3 Include:

  • the name of the requesting agency;
  • the name, title and contact details of the requesting official, including an official email address and phone number;
  • the legal authority for the request, with a copy of the legal process;
  • the account identifier: the email address on the account, or the workspace ID;
  • the specific information requested; and
  • the time range.

18.4 We accept legal process from US law-enforcement and government agencies by email to contact@parafield.ai, if it comes from an official government email address. Accepting process this way does not waive any objection, including objections to jurisdiction or to how the process was served. Civil subpoenas and other process from private parties must be served in a way the law allows, such as on our registered agent. We do not accept civil process by email unless we agree in writing.

19. Changes to these guidelines

We may update these guidelines. The version published at /law-enforcement is the current one.
